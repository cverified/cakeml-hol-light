# Theorems whose statement the port does not have

Phase two states every theorem of the sources with its name, its
attributes and its statement (`CLAUDE.md`).  Of the 15,546
theorems of the 445 sources the port states **14,758** and points
at **788** with a comment that names them
(`CAKE_ALL=1 python3 -I tools/coverage.py`; every one of the 788
has such a pointer):

| | a pointer |
|---|---|
| `ThmValue` (the value of an ML expression) | 443 |
| a theorem that ML code states (`Q.prove`) | 178 |
| `Theorem` (and `Triviality`) | 167 |

**The 788 are one thing, not three.**  In each of them HOL4
*computes* the statement instead of writing it, so there is no
text in the source to read off.  The `Theorem` row is an
antiquotation of a term that the ML of the source builds:

```
Theorem comp_correct:
  ^(ind_thm |> concl |> rand)
```

and the other two shapes are the same idea under another name --
`Theorem foo = SIMP_RULE bool_ss [] bar` computes the statement
from another theorem, and a `Q.prove` inside ML states one that
the ML built.  So the question for all 788 is the same: can the
port **do the computation** that HOL4 does?

`tools/derive.py` is where that happens: it translates the ML
expression into a HOL Light one, which is why the port has 14,758
and not fewer.  `tools/warnings.txt` logs every derivation it
gives up on, and the reasons are a long tail: `not a name`, a
`Once` in a rewrite list, then dozens of one-offs, and a set that
are **cascades**, where the derivation names a theorem that is
itself one of the 788.  Each cause is a small addition to
`derive.py` and each may unlock several theorems at once.

The 167 of the `Theorem` row were 7 before the port took in
blocks A and B: the translator, the basis library and Candle
state far more of their theorems this way than the compiler's
backend does, because each of them is generated by ML that walks
a datatype.

Two of them would not come across even if the ML did run, and
they are the exception to the paragraph above:

- `bvl_const$SmartOp2_pmatch` states its `pmatch` form, which is
  HOL4's `patternMatches` library; the port writes `match` and
  has no counterpart to state.
- `flatProps$v_size_aux` relates `v1_size` to `v4_size`, the
  auxiliary size functions HOL4 generates for a mutually
  recursive datatype.  HOL Light's size machinery handles lists
  and pairs generically and makes two functions where HOL4 makes
  five, so the equations have nothing to be about.

# Definitions whose body the port does not have

Of the 6,586 definition, datatype and inductive blocks of the 445
sources the port makes **6,574**.  The 24 blocks that are out
stand in eleven files, each as a pointer at the HOL4 source
(`grep -rn 'PORT FAILED' ml/`), and they are three causes:

- **HOL4 computes the body with ML code** (16 blocks, in
  `to_word32Prog`, `to_word64Prog`, `compiler32Prog`,
  `compiler64Prog`, `repl_init` and `backend_cv`).  This is the
  same thing as the 788 above, for a definition instead of a
  statement:

  ```
  Definition int_to_words_def:
    int_to_words c i offset =
      ^(part_to_words_def |> CONJUNCTS |> el 1
       |> SPEC_ALL |> concl |> rand)
  ```

  `tools/overrides.py`'s `ANTIQUOTE` is where such a value is
  given when the port cannot compute it, keyed by the text of the
  ML expression.

- **a notation of HOL4 that the port does not declare** (4
  blocks).  `basis_ffi`'s three `whole_prog_spec`s and
  `TextIOProof`'s `sub_spec` are written in the separation logic
  of `characteristic/`, whose `app`, `SEP_EXISTS` and `POSTv` are
  HOL4 mixfixes; `holSemantics`'s `is_std_interpretation` uses
  `_ interprets _ on _ as _`, a mixfix of `holSemanticsExtra`.
  Each is a fixity to declare before the file reads.

- **a term quotation inside an ML block** (2 blocks).
  `DoubleProg`'s `posmin64` and `poszero64` antiquote a term that
  the ML writes with HOL4's Unicode quotation marks.

# Overloads that HOL4 resolves by type

HOL4 may declare one name as several overloads, each at a type of
its own, and its parser picks the one that typechecks.  The port
keeps one value per overload and writes it everywhere, so where a
source leans on that resolution the port says the wrong thing.

Two families of the scope do:

- **Candle's signatures.**  `holSyntax` declares `tysof`, `tmsof`,
  `sigof` and `axsof` more than once -- `FST` of a signature, the
  same after `sigof` for a theory, and a fold over a context.
  Which one a place means is what it is applied to, and
  `overrides.HOLSYNTAX_SIGS` says so for the three shapes that a
  bare variable takes (`sig`, `thy`, `ctxt`).  What is left is the
  shape where one of them is applied to *another* of them --
  `term_ok (sigof (thyof ctxt)) tm` -- which the patterns do not
  reach.  Six statements of `holSyntaxExtra` and a handful
  elsewhere.

- **setSpec's comma.**  `Overload "," = ``pair ^mem``` makes the
  comma the pair of the set theory where both sides are elements
  of `'U`, and the tuple of HOL otherwise.  One definition of the
  file needed saying (`is_model`); the theorems write the triple
  themselves.

The general answer to both is to resolve an overload by the type,
which is what HOL4 does and what the port's one-value-per-name
tables cannot.  Short of that, each shape is a pattern.

# HOL4's rationals

`ml/lib/rat.ml` has the type `rat` and nine operations on it
**declared** and not defined (`cake_declare`, so no axiom), and
`rat_minv_def` is a pointer at HOL4's definition.  HOL4 builds a
rational as a quotient of `fracTheory`'s fractions, which are a
quotient of pairs of integers, and the port has neither theory.

Nothing of CakeML reaches into a rational -- `mlrat` and
`RatProg` use `rat_of_int`, `rat_minv` and `rat_compare`, and say
the rest through the operations -- so the statements read against
the declarations and are the statements HOL4 makes.

What it would take to define them: carve the rationals out of HOL
Light's reals (`?p q. ~(q = 0) /\ x = &p / &q`) with
`new_type_definition`, and lift the nine operations through the
bijection.  Each lifting needs the closure of the rationals under
that operation, so it is a type definition and six proofs, which
is phase three work.

# What the port has of the ML code of a source

A source is not only its blocks: 2,435 blocks of the 270 are the
ML code of HOL4 between them, and this is what the port has of
each.

- **the ML of a library theory** (611): of a
  library theory the port has the definitions that CakeML needs,
  and nothing else is in.
- **the bookkeeping of HOL4** (183): a parse option, a
  `temp_delsimps`, an `export_theory`, an `add_record_field`.
  There is nothing in it for the port to have (`IGNORE_ML` in
  `tools/port.py` is the list).
- **the ML that states a theorem** (75): a `val foo =
  Q.prove(...)` of a source is a theorem named `foo`.  Phase two
  states 35 of them, where the quotation is what the theorem
  says, and points at the other 40, where a rule of HOL4 makes
  something else of it (`|> SIMP_RULE ...`).
- **the ML that the port has in OCaml** (63): the code that
  builds the deep embedding of `word_bignum`, the monadic
  machinery of `reg_alloc` and `linear_scan`, the grammar of
  `gram` and of `simpleSexpPEG`, and the terms that
  `flat_to_clos` builds with `check1`.  `ML_PORTED` and
  `EXTRA_BEFORE` in `tools/overrides.py` say where the OCaml
  goes, and it takes the place of the blocks that it covers.
- **everything else** (1,544): a pointer at the lines of the
  source says what is there (a proof, the ML values that the
  definitions below antiquote, or ML code that is not ported).
  `tools/nohol4.py` checks that every pointer points at what it
  says.

# The last two blocks the port cannot write

Of the seventeen `PORT FAILED` blocks of 2026-11-16 two are left,
and each is one line of grammar or one ML value away:

- `holSemantics$is_std_interpretation` says

      tmaof i interprets <<=>> on [<<A>>] as
        \l. (Abstract (HD l) (Funspace (HD l) boolset) ...)

  and the port stops at the `\` (`unparsed input at 137`).
  HOL4's `interprets _ on _ as _` takes a lambda as its last
  operand, and the port's parser will not start one after an

  wants measuring: the last change to the printer's scope rules
  rewrote 102 files before it was narrowed to 6.

- `repl_init$repl_prog_st` is `^(th2 |> concl |> rand)`, and
  `th2` is a theorem the ML of the source builds and rebinds as
  it goes (`repl_initScript.sml:26` rewrites it with the very
  definition this block makes).  `tools/derive.py` can follow the
  chain; what it has no value for is `th2`.

# An overload expanded inside a binder

`holBoolSyntax` heads the largest group of the files that still
fail, and the cause is found.  The failing block is `ExistsDef`,
and the port writes a lambda whose **binders are terms**:

    (\(holSyntax_Var (implode "x") (holSyntax_Tyvar (implode "A")))
       ty (holSyntax_Var (implode "p") ...). ...)

with `holSyntax_Var (holSyntax_Var (implode "x") ...) ty` inside
it -- a `Var` whose name argument is a `Var`, which is exactly
the error the file reports (`holSyntax_Var ... cannot be used
with type mlstring->type->mlstring`).

The source (`holBoolSyntaxScript.sml:18`) has

    Overload Exists = "\x ty p. Comb (Const <<?>> ...) (Abs (Var x ty) p)"

and the same file overloads the names `x` and `p` themselves
(`:24-33`).  The port expands an overload wherever the name
stands -- including where it is the **binder** of a lambda, which
is not a name to resolve at all.  So the fix is in the
translation: a bound variable is bound, and no table speaks for
it.

**Half of it is fixed.**  `parse_binders` marks what it binds and
the printer leaves a marked name alone, so the binders read
`\x ty p.` again (six files changed, `42ff165`).  What is left is
the **body**: `Abs (Var x ty) p` still comes out as
`holSyntax_Abs (holSyntax_Var (holSyntax_Var (implode "x") ...) ty)
...`, because `x` there is a *use* of the variable the lambda
binds, and the printer expands it with the file's
`Overload x = Var <<x>> A` all the same.

So the printer has to know what is in scope, not only what is
being bound.  The cheapest way in is `ctx.vars`, which the
printer already consults **before** the overload table: a lambda
or a quantifier can put its binder names into it, mapped to
themselves, while its body is printed.  Thirteen occurrences in
holBoolSyntax alone.

What was ruled out first, so that nobody looks there again: the
statement of `TrueDef` parses in a session that holds the whole
port, in one that is stopped in front of the file, and under the
file's own `cake_theory` scope; `===`, `holSyntax_Abs` and
`implode` all have the types they should in all three.  The first
four blocks of the file load; it is the fifth that does not.

# The Silver machine model

`tools/excluded.txt` leaves `ag32` and `ag32_target` out, because
`ag32Script.sml` is a file that L3 generated: it builds its types
and its definitions by calling the ML of HOL4's `Import` library,
which a source level port cannot follow (`ISSUES.md`).  That was
written for the encoder, and the encoder is indeed only reached
through `ag32_config`, which the port declares.

But **seven files of the load order use the model itself** and
cannot load without it:

    compiler/backend/ag32/ag32_memory.ml
    compiler/backend/ag32/proofs/ag32_prog.ml
    compiler/backend/ag32/proofs/ag32_machine_config.ml
    compiler/backend/ag32/proofs/ag32_memoryProof.ml
    compiler/backend/ag32/proofs/ag32_basis_ffiProof.ml
    compiler/bootstrap/compilation/ag32/32/proofs/ag32BootstrapProof.ml
    compiler/encoders/ag32/proofs/ag32_targetProof.ml

They used to report what the port makes of a name it does not
have: `ag32_memory` said `, has type A->B->A#B, it cannot be used
with type ?->?->lab`, because `Jump (fSnd,0w,Reg 0w)` is ag32's
`Jump` of one triple and the port wrote labLang's `Jump` of one
label; the proofs said `unknown type ag32_state`.

What those files use of the model is **data and nineteen names**,
not the 29 definitions, and `ml/lib/ag32.ml` now has that much of
it by hand: the datatypes `funcT`, `shiftT`, `reg_immT` and
`instruction` (`ag32Script.sml:6-38`), the record `ag32_state`
(`:40-45`), the fifteen `dfn'…` step functions that the proofs of
the memory layout state theorems about, and `Encode`, `Decode`,
`Run` and `Next`.  The nineteen are **declared without a value**,
because their value is the generated ML -- which is what keeps
them on this page.  (`incPC`, the twentieth name those files
write, is only in a tactic.)

The names are the ones `db.mapping()` gives the theory once its
datatypes are in `overrides.EXTRA_DATATYPES` (and its record in
`EXTRA_RECORDS`), so that what the port writes and what the file
makes are the same constants.  Adding them moves no other
theory's name, but it does give `asm`'s `Imm` and `Reg` their
prefix in eighty files: the port has one namespace, and once
`ag32` has an `Imm` of its own, `asm`'s cannot keep the bare
name.

**None.**  Fifteen of the definitions of `ml/CHEATS.md` were once

| what | why |
|---|---|
| `list_size f (BUTLAST l) <= list_size f l` and `~(l = []) ==> f (LAST l) <= list_size f l` in `cake_term_facts` | the `Let` clause recurses on the list without its last expression and on that last expression, and `ml/lib/define.ml` invites a ported file to add the facts that its own definitions ask for (`overrides.EXTRA_BEFORE` adds them, `EXTRA_AFTER` takes them back) |
| a budget of more than eight seconds | eight is what a definition of the port gets before it is assumed instead, and these two take about twenty each |
| the relation handed to `define_single` rather than to `cake_define_gen` (`overrides.DEFINE_REL`) | the chain of `cake_define_gen` tries eight strategies before the relation, one of them declares the constant and then fails, and `with Failure _ when not (declared ())` tries nothing after that -- so the relation never got its turn |

With the three in place `compiler/backend/bvi_let.ml` adds **no
axiom**: both constants come from `new_specification` and their
clauses are theorems.  It took a wrong diagnosis first, and that
one is in `PORTING-GUIDE.md` entry 17, because the way it went
wrong is the part worth keeping.
